Executive Definition

A Non-Performing Asset (NPA) is a loan or advance for which the principal or interest payment remained overdue for a continuous period of 90 days, ceasing to generate yield for the bank.

Primary Vectors

NPAs are typically driven by poor individual repayment history, a disproportionately high principal burden relative to income, or broader systemic macroeconomic instability across sectors.

Banking Impact

High NPA ratios directly erode institutional profitability, force mandatory capital provisioning, limit further credit expansion, and ultimately threaten depositor confidence.

Real-World Case Example

A retail borrower is issued a structural loan but experiences unexpected financial distress. The borrower fails to satisfy scheduled EMI repayments for three consecutive months (90 days). Consequently, the core banking system automatically reclassifies the loan from a "Standard Asset" to a Non-Performing Asset. The bank is now forced by the RBI to provision regulatory capital from its profits to cover the anticipated loss.
